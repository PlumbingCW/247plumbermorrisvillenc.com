Sump Pump Float Repair in Morrisville: Test, Free or Replace.

A sump pump float switch that sticks, tangles or wears out is a common reason a pump will not start or will not shut off. You can test it in a few minutes, free it if it is jammed, and replace the switch alone when the pump motor still runs.

This applies only to homes that have a sump pit. Many Morrisville homes do not have one, and if yours does not, the guide below is background reading, not a to-do list.

By the Morrisville 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-30

First, does your home even have a sump pump?

A sump pump sits in a pit, usually in a basement or low utility area, and pumps groundwater away from the foundation. Not every house has one. We have not found a reliable local source on which foundation types are typical in Morrisville, so we will not guess.

Check your own home. Look for a round plastic or concrete pit with a lid in a basement, garage or utility room, plus a discharge pipe leaving the house. If you do not see one, you can skip the rest of this page. If you want to know whether water is a risk at your address, see basement waterproofing.

What a float switch does.

The float is the part that tells the pump when to run. It rises with the water in the pit and closes a switch at the top level, then drops and opens the switch at the bottom. Two styles are common. A tethered float swings on a cord and a vertical float slides up and down a rod.

Because the float lives in water, it collects debris, soap and sediment, and it can wedge against the pit wall or the discharge pipe. A float that cannot move is the same as a switch that has failed, and the pump will behave the same way.

Test the float, safely.

Always unplug the pump before you reach into the pit. Unplug the float switch cord from the piggyback plug, then plug the pump cord directly into the wall outlet. If the pump runs, the motor is fine and the float switch is the likely fault. Do not leave it running that way for more than a few minutes, because a dry pump overheats.

A second test uses a non-conducting stick, such as a broom handle. Lift the float to its on position while the pump is plugged back in through the switch. The pump should start. If it does not, the switch or its wiring is at fault.

A practical order for the check.

  1. 01

    Cut power.

    Unplug the pump and float. Never work in a wet pit with the pump powered.

    .
  2. 02

    Look for a jam.

    Remove the lid and check for stones, debris or a wedged float. Make sure a tethered float has at least about 3 inches of cord free between the ball and its bracket.

    .
  3. 03

    Run the direct test.

    Plug the pump directly into the wall for a few seconds and confirm it pumps water out of the pit.

    .
  4. 04

    Decide repair or replace.

    If the pump runs directly, replace or free the float. If it does not, the pump needs service or replacement.

    .

When to free it, replace it or replace the pump.

Rules of thumb that save money.

  • Free the float and clean the pit if debris was the cause and the switch clicks normally.
  • Replace only the float switch when the pump runs on the direct test and the old switch is worn or sticky.
  • Replace the whole pump when the motor hums without pumping, trips the breaker, or the unit has been running poorly for years.
  • Consider a pump with a vertical switch or an electronic switch if the pit is narrow and a tethered float keeps tangling.

Washing machine or laundry water changes the choice. A pump that handles laundry discharge should be an effluent style with a tethered switch, because soap scum can lock a vertical float in place. Our sump pump repair page covers installs and replacements.

Local flood facts and why backup power matters.

Why a Triangle sump pump needs power during big storms.

The Wake County Hazard Mitigation Plan, adopted in October 2024, records 231 flood events across the county between 1996 and 2023. Morrisville appears in the plan's flood event list. When Hurricane Fran hit in September 1996, the National Weather Service recorded 8.80 inches of rain and a 79 mph gust at Raleigh-Durham airport, and about 1.5 million people lost power across the state.

The practical lesson is simple. A sump pump only works when it has power, so a float that is perfect on a dry day does nothing during an outage. If you have a pit, test the float before storm season and ask a plumber about a battery backup pump as a second line of defense.

Sources: Wake County Hazard Mitigation Plan and the National Weather Service Hurricane Fran summary.

Standards and safety around a sump pit.

Replacement pumps are plumbing work under the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Treat the pit as an electrical hazard: unplug the pump before you reach in.
