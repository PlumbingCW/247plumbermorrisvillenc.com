Know your cleanout before you need it.

A sewer backup is easier to sort out when you know where the cleanout is. These four checks take ten minutes.

  1. 01

    Find the cleanout.

    It is a capped pipe at ground level, often near the house or toward the edge of the lot. Do not remove the cap if water is rising.

    .
  2. 02

    Note which fixtures are slow.

    One slow sink points to a branch line. Several fixtures gurgling together point to the main line.

    .
  3. 03

    Stop adding water.

    Skip the dishwasher, laundry and long showers until a plumber has looked.

    .
  4. 04

    Call and describe it.

    Tell dispatch what is slow, what you tried and whether sewage is visible.

    .

Where Cary stops and you start.

Sewer responsibility in Morrisville.

The Town of Cary provides water and sewer service for Morrisville residents. Cary's published practice is to maintain the main and the connection up to the cleanout at the edge of your property, and the owner is responsible from the cleanout to the house.

That line decides who to call. If the blockage is in the street, call Cary at (919) 469-4000 and say you are outside Cary town limits. If it is between the cleanout and your home, it is a plumbing job done to the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code (based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code, IPC), and sewer line cleaning or a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening.

Sources: Town of Morrisville new residents page, Town of Cary utility account services.

Why the same drain keeps clogging.

A drain that clears and then clogs again usually has a cause that a plunger never reaches. Grease poured down a kitchen sink, wipes flushed in a bathroom and roots that find a joint in an older yard line are the usual suspects. A camera shows which one you have, and hydro jetting can scour a line that cable clearing only opens briefly.

Ask the plumber to show you the footage, and ask where the cleanout is so the next visit starts faster.

Other calls we expect here.

Not every problem is a sewer problem. A dripping faucet or leaking valve and a jammed disposal are among the everyday calls, and a leaking tank is a case for water heater replacement. In a townhome section such as Everett Crossing, your association may set its own rules for exterior work, so ask before you start.
