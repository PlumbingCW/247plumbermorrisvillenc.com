Slow drain, gurgle or standing water: what each sign points to.

Where the water misbehaves tells you more than how bad it looks. One slow bathroom sink usually means hair and soap scum in the trap or the first few feet of branch line. A tub that gurgles when the washer empties points farther down, where several fixtures share one pipe.

Water rising in the lowest tub or a floor drain while a toilet flushes is the classic sign of a blocked main drain. That one needs a plumber the same day.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food scraps and the disposal.

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom ones. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe, coats the wall, and then catches rice, coffee grounds and peels until the opening is the size of a pencil. A two inch kitchen line can look fine at the trap and be nearly shut a few feet into the wall.

A disposal that hums but leaves water standing is a different repair from a clogged line. If the unit itself is jammed or leaking, see garbage disposal repair. If water backs into the second sink bowl or the dishwasher hose, the clog is downstream and a cable run should clear it.

Shower and tub drains that stay slow.

Hair and soap cause most of these. Hair wraps around the pop-up stopper linkage or the strainer crossbar, then gathers soap into a plug you can sometimes lift out in one piece. We pull the stopper first, because a hand tool often finishes the job.

A tub that drains fine alone but slows when the bathroom sink runs is describing the branch line, not the stopper. Fixing that means going past the tub trap, usually through the overflow plate or a nearby cleanout.

Grease, wipes and the town sewer in Morrisville.

Morrisville's sewer service comes from the Town of Cary, and Cary runs a fats, oils and grease control program. Its curbside cooking oil collection is for Cary solid-waste customers only. Morrisville trash and recycling runs through GFL under the Town, so a Morrisville kitchen should use the alternative Cary lists: drop used oil at a Wake County Multi-Material Recycling Facility.

That ties straight back to your kitchen line. Let the pan cool, pour the oil into a lidded can, wipe the skillet with a paper towel before washing, and take the can to a county site when it fills. Managers of apartments and townhomes can ask Cary's pretreatment program about collection events.

A wet cabinet: clog or leaking drain line?

A drain that weeps is leaking, not clogged, and cabling it will not help. Run water for a minute, then feel under the sink. Drips at the slip nuts on the trap point to a loose or worn connection, while moisture on the wall pipe or the cabinet floor suggests a crack behind the wall.

A leak that appears only while the fixture is draining belongs to the drain side, not the supply side. Our leak detection visit separates the two, so you do not pay to replace the wrong pipe.

What not to pour, push or plunge.

Common moves that make a clog worse.

  • Caustic or acid drain openers in a fully blocked pipe. The chemical sits there until a person has to work on it.
  • A plunger on a double kitchen bowl without plugging the second side. The pressure just pushes through to the other basin.
  • A wire hanger or garden hose pushed into a tub or toilet trap. It scratches porcelain and snags in the bend.
  • Two cleaners mixed together. Some pairs release a toxic gas in a closed bathroom.
  • Wipes sold as flushable. They do not break up like paper and gather at joints downstream.

When a cable is not the right tool.

A cable opens a hole through a clog but does not scrub the pipe wall. If the same drain slows again within weeks, the line likely carries a coat of grease or scale that the cable punched through and left in place. That is the case for hydro jetting, which washes the full circumference.

If every fixture is slow, or the blockage sits in the pipe that runs to the street, read about sewer line cleaning next.

Safety checklist before you clear a drain yourself.

Safety steps for a clogged drain.

  • Do not mix drain cleaners, and do not use a chemical cleaner after another one has failed.
  • Do not plunge a drain that has standing chemical cleaner in it. Splashback burns skin and eyes.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection for any snake or plunger work.
  • Do not run a snake into a drain that is backing up with sewage. Stop and call.
  • If several fixtures back up together, shut off water use and call a plumber.

Pipe repairs that follow a clog fall under the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Cary's guidelines on fats, oils and grease are the local rule of thumb for what never goes down a kitchen drain.

What changes the price of drain cleaning.

No one can responsibly quote a clog over the phone, so here is what moves the price once a plumber has seen the line.

Access point.

A trap we can pull and a cleanout nearby make a simple job. A line reachable only through a roof vent or by lifting a toilet takes longer.

How far the clog sits.

A clog at the trap is quick. One forty feet down a three or four inch line needs more cable, more time and a heavier machine.

What the clog is made of.

Hair and soap clear differently from roots, grease or a dropped toy. Roots and scale may call for a different cutting head or for jetting.

Repeat clogs.

A line that keeps closing usually needs a camera look to find the cause, which adds a step.

Fixture removal.

Pulling and resetting a toilet to reach the line adds labor and a new wax ring.

Insurers usually treat a gradual clog as maintenance, so the clearing itself is rarely covered. Water damage from a sudden drain or sewer backup is paid only if your policy carries a backup endorsement, so check yours. You get a clear price before work starts.
