Floor drains and drain lines.

Commercial floor drains are easy to forget. A trap that dries out lets sewer gas up into the room, and a clogged branch can back up into a mop sink or a restroom. We clear them with a cable or with hydro jetting when grease or scale is the cause.

A camera is the next step if the same drain keeps clogging. It shows whether the problem is grease, a bellied line or something in the pipe, and our sewer inspection page explains what a camera visit involves.

Backflow testing, honestly.

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps water from flowing backward into the building supply. It sits on irrigation, fire lines, boilers and some commercial supply lines. Over time its internal check valves wear, and a failed assembly can let used water back into the clean side.

Many water systems require periodic testing, and results usually go to the utility. We do not quote a schedule from memory because the requirement depends on the utility and the device. Ask the water utility serving your address, which for Morrisville is the Town of Cary, what testing it asks for and who is accepted to test. Durham addresses answer to Durham's Cross Connection Control Department, which issues its own permit for backflow installs.

A test is simple. The tester attaches a gauge set, closes the shutoffs in sequence and records pressure readings across each check valve. A passing assembly is documented. A failing one gets new seats or rubber parts, or a replacement, and is retested.

Grease trap and interceptor cleaning.

Fats, oils and grease and the Cary sewer.

Morrisville sewage flows into the Town of Cary system, and Cary runs a fats, oils and grease control program. Its pretreatment staff are the people to ask which rules apply to a food business at your address, because requirements differ between a small cafe and a full-service restaurant.

The only Cary number we can point to is the Utility Pretreatment Program Supervisor at (919) 319-4564, which Cary lists for apartment and townhome managers asking about collection events. Call it, say you are in Morrisville, and ask who handles commercial kitchens. Keep your pump-out records so you can show them if asked.

Source: Town of Cary, fats, oils and grease control

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the sewer. Cleaning means pumping out the contents, scraping the walls, checking the baffles and inspecting the lid gasket. Waiting too long makes a full unit pass grease into the building drain, which leads to the slow kitchen drains described above.

We can clean and inspect small under-sink traps and help with larger interceptor service, and we are straightforward about hauling. Larger in-ground tanks usually need a pump truck and a licensed hauler, and we will tell you if the job needs one.

Codes and testing for business plumbing.

Commercial work is built to the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code, which rests on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and has been mandatory since July 1, 2025. Backflow assemblies and grease management carry their own local rules, and we do not assume a schedule or a requirement for your building.

Ask the Town of Morrisville Inspections Department at 919-463-6190 what your occupancy requires, and ask Cary who handles commercial kitchens in your area.

Storm drains and drainage on the property.

Storm drains are not sewers.

A storm drain carries rainwater to creeks and not to a treatment plant. Rainfall at Raleigh-Durham airport runs about 46 inches a year (NWS RDU 1991-2020 daily normals), so parking lots and roof leaders get a lot of work. Leaves, mulch and silt settle in catch basins and slow the flow.

We clear private catch basins, area drains and downspout lines on a property. Public storm drains belong to the town. To report dumping into a storm drain or stream, the Town of Morrisville Stormwater hotline is 919-463-SOAK (7025). Never wash grease, paint or cleaning water into a storm drain.

Source: Town of Morrisville, stormwater resources

Commercial leak detection.

A building that loses water without anyone noticing shows it on the meter and the bill. We start by isolating zones with the building's own valves, then use acoustic listening gear, a camera and a pressure test on suspect lines to narrow the source. Details on the tools are on our leak detection page.

We keep our promises small. Some leaks can be located precisely and some can only be narrowed to a section of slab or wall, and we say which one before any opening is cut.
