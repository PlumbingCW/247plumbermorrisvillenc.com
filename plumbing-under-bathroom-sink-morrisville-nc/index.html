Plumbing Under a Bathroom Sink in Morrisville: Every Part, and Where It Leaks.

The plumbing under a bathroom sink is two short water lines coming in and one curved drain going out. The supply lines carry hot and cold from the wall valves to the faucet, and the P-trap holds a small plug of water that blocks sewer gas from the room.

Most leaks in a Morrisville, NC bathroom cabinet start at one of about six joints. Once you know the parts, you can find a drip in minutes and decide whether it is a quick tightening or a job for a plumber.

By the Morrisville 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-07

The parts, from the wall to the drain.

Under-sink parts and what each one does.
Part.What it does.Common failure.
Angle stop valves.Shut off hot or cold to this sink only.Slow drip at the stem, or stuck and will not close.
Supply lines.Braided hoses from stop to faucet.Leak at the nut, frayed or kinked braid.
Pop-up assembly.Lever and stopper at the drain.Slow leak at the nut or a stopper that will not seal.
Tailpiece.Short vertical pipe from the drain down to the trap.Loose slip nut.
P-trap.Curved pipe that holds water as a seal.Leak at slip nuts, clog, or cracked bowl.
Trap arm and wall stub.Carries waste into the wall drain.Poor slope, loose escutcheon, corrosion at a metal stub.

Between the water lines you also find the faucet's mounting nuts and, on some sinks, a small overflow channel inside the basin that connects to the drain below. A leak that appears only when the basin is nearly full often comes from that overflow path.

How to find a leak in under five minutes.

  1. 01

    Empty and dry the cabinet.

    Take everything out and wipe the base so a fresh drip is easy to see.

    .
  2. 02

    Wrap dry paper towel.

    Wrap each joint with a dry paper towel, one at a time, and run the water briefly.

    .
  3. 03

    Check supply, then drain.

    Open the tap for hot and cold and look at the supply nuts. Then fill the basin and release it to test the drain joints.

    .
  4. 04

    Look up, not just down.

    A drip from the faucet body or the mounting nuts runs down the pipe and shows up lower than it started.

    .

Run the test in that order. Supply leaks show up while the water is running at the tap, and drain leaks show up when you release the basin. That tells you which set of joints to check.

The P-trap, and why it is shaped that way.

The curve keeps a few inches of water standing in the pipe. Sewer gas cannot pass through it, which is why a bathroom that sits unused for weeks can start to smell when the trap dries out. Run the tap for a minute and the smell often goes.

Slip nuts on a trap use a washer that seals when snug. Hand tightening plus a quarter turn is usually right. Cranking hard cracks plastic nuts.

A slow drain is often the same cabinet.

Hair and soap film collect in the pop-up assembly and at the bottom of the trap. Pull the stopper, clean it, and check the trap with a bucket under it. That fixes most sluggish sinks.

If water still backs up after cleaning, the blockage is farther down the line. That is when a drain cleaning visit with a small cable machine makes sense, rather than pouring chemicals into a pipe you can no longer see.

Morrisville tap water and your faucet.

Cary's 2025 water testing summary lists the hardness of the water it supplies, which serves Morrisville, at about 30 mg/L as calcium carbonate, classed as moderately soft. In practice that means heavy mineral crust on a bathroom aerator is less common here than in hard-water areas.

So if you see white crust building around a faucet base or a supply nut, treat it as a clue to check for a slow seep at that joint, not as normal scale.

Source: Cary 2025 Drinking Water Detailed Report, checked October 2026.

Repairs you can do and repairs to hand off.

Reasonable to try yourself.

  • Tighten a slip nut a quarter turn and re-test.
  • Replace a braided supply line, after closing the stop valve.
  • Clean the pop-up stopper and the trap bowl.
  • Replace a worn faucet aerator.

Call a plumber when.

  • The angle stop will not close or drips from its stem.
  • The wall stub is corroded, or the cabinet floor is swollen.
  • A leak returns after you replace the washer or hose.
  • Water comes out of the wall, not from a visible joint.

For faucet bodies, cartridges and stops, see our faucet and sink repair service. A stop valve that will not shut is more than a nuisance, because it is your emergency shutoff for the whole sink.

Corrosion and old parts.

Chrome-plated brass and steel traps can corrode from the inside, and the first sign is a damp patch at the bottom bend. Plastic traps do not corrode but yellow with age and crack at the threads.

If a metal trap has gone green or white at the joints, replace the whole assembly, not just a washer. Mixing metals at a wall stub can start galvanic corrosion, so ask a plumber to match materials.

Under-sink storage that causes leaks.

A cabinet full of cleaning bottles hides a leak for months. Wet cardboard, a swollen particleboard floor and a musty smell are the usual clues. Keep a small shelf or a plastic tray in the cabinet so a drip lands somewhere visible.

Check the flexible supply lines every year or two. Braided stainless hoses outlast rubber ones, but the fittings still wear. A hose with a bulge or a rust bloom at the nut should be swapped before it fails.

When the sink is not the only problem.

If the sink drains slowly and so do the tub and a second bathroom, the problem is not in the cabinet. A shared branch line or main drain is partly blocked. Our drain cleaning team uses a cable machine or hydro jetting depending on what the line holds.

Quick reference before you call.

Have these ready.

  • Whether the leak shows with water running, or only when the basin drains.
  • Whether the angle stops close fully.
  • The age and material of the trap, metal or plastic.
  • A photo of the joint that looks wet.

Code notes for what sits under a sink.

The traps, supply valves and drain lines under a sink are installed to the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Repairs that stay like-for-like are usually simple, but any rerouting is worth a plumber's eye.

Check for corrosion on the shutoff valves and replace them if they do not close fully.
