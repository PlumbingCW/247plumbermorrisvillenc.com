Installing a New Wax Ring on a Toilet in Morrisville: When and How.

Replace a toilet's wax ring any time the toilet comes up off the floor, and also when you see water at the base, a toilet that rocks, or a faint sewer smell in the bathroom. The ring is a one-time seal and it should never be reused.

This guide walks through the full job in order, from shutting off the supply to setting the bowl, and it covers the flange problems that make a fresh ring fail. It also tells you when the job is better left to a licensed plumber.

By the Morrisville 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-30

Signs the wax ring has failed.

A wax ring seals the joint between the toilet outlet and the drain flange on the floor. When it fails, wastewater leaks out each time you flush, and sewer gas can drift up around the base. The leak is usually small and slow, so the first sign is often a stain or a soft spot in the floor.

Water that appears only after a flush points to the ring. Water that is present all day, even between flushes, usually points to the supply line, the shutoff valve or condensation on the tank instead.

Gather the tools and materials first.

What the job needs.

  • A new wax ring sized for your drain, or a wax-free seal if the flange is awkward.
  • New closet bolts and a pair of plastic bolt caps, because the old bolts are often corroded.
  • A putty knife and a rag for scraping the old wax off the flange and the bowl outlet.
  • A bucket, a sponge and a small cup to remove the last water from the bowl and tank.
  • A level, an adjustable wrench and, if the flange is low, a flange spacer ring.

Step by step: pulling and resetting the toilet.

Take your time with the lift and the set. Most failed jobs are failed at the set.

  1. 01

    Shut off, flush and drain.

    Close the supply valve, flush to empty the tank, then sponge out what is left in the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line.

    .
  2. 02

    Unbolt and lift straight up.

    Remove the caps and nuts on the closet bolts, rock the toilet gently to break the wax seal and lift it off the flange. Set it on newspaper or a towel, and bend your knees because a porcelain toilet is heavy and awkward.

    .
  3. 03

    Scrape and inspect.

    Remove all old wax from the flange and the horn on the bowl. Stuff a rag in the drain opening to block sewer gas while you work.

    .
  4. 04

    Set the new ring and bowl.

    Fit the new ring on the flange or the bowl horn, lower the toilet straight down over the bolts and press it down evenly. Do not rock it side to side after it touches the ring.

    .

Tighten the nuts a little at a time, alternating sides, until the toilet is snug and level. Overtightening cracks porcelain. Reconnect the supply line, open the valve, let the tank fill and flush several times while you watch the base for any seepage.

Why a new ring can still leak: check the flange.

The flange is the ring bolted to the floor at the top of the drain pipe. If it sits below the finished floor, the ring cannot compress enough to seal. A cracked or broken flange, or one that has pulled loose from the subfloor, will leak no matter how new the wax is.

A flange should sit on top of the finished floor surface or flush with it. If your bathroom floor was tiled after the flange was installed, the flange may now be a half inch or more too low. A flange spacer or a thicker ring can bridge a small gap, while a rotted or broken flange needs a proper repair.

Look for a rusted metal flange, a cracked plastic flange or a flange that moves when you push on it. These are the repairs where a plumber's help is worth it. Our toilet repair and installation service covers flange repair and resets.

Cary meter and dye checks to rule out other leaks.

Rule out a running toilet before you pull it.

Water and sewer service for Morrisville homes comes from the Town of Cary, and Cary's high-water-consumption page describes two quick checks. Lift your meter cover and look at the leak indicator while no water is in use. If it moves, you may have a leak somewhere. For the toilet itself, add food coloring to the tank, wait without flushing, and look for color in the bowl.

Neither check will flag a failed wax ring, because that leak happens only during a flush and drains downstream of the fixture. The checks tell you whether a running tank or a hidden supply leak is the real problem. Billing and leak-adjustment questions go to Cary at 311 or (919) 469-4000 from outside Cary town limits.

Source: Town of Cary high water consumption tips.

If the meter does show movement with everything off, you may have an active supply leak somewhere else in the home. A leak detection visit finds that without opening walls.

Signs the job is bigger than a wax ring.

  • The subfloor around the toilet is soft, stained or smells musty after you lift the bowl.
  • The flange is cracked, rusted through or loose on the floor.
  • The toilet leaks again within days of the reset.
  • You find the drain line itself is slow or backs up, since that points to a clog downstream.
