Plumber in Carpenter Park, Morrisville, NC.

Carpenter Park is a named area in Morrisville that the Town lists as both single-family homes and multi-family buildings. The Town's lists also tie Preston Creekside condominiums to the Carpenter Park entry.

The first question on any call is whether you own a house or a condo, so say so up front and call (919) 944-7281.

What the Town lists for Carpenter Park.

The Town's residency district page puts Carpenter Park in its fourth district along with Addison Park, Ridgemont, Savannah, Grace Park and Downing Glen. Its trash route lists show Carpenter Park single-family streets on a Tuesday route, with at least one street on Thursday, and multi-family buildings served by dumpster instead of curbside carts.

Streets the lists attach to Carpenter Park include Millet Drive, Rama Court and Madres Lane. We found no published boundary map, so confirm your address with the Town if you are unsure which entry applies to you.

Association documents differ, so we do not assume where your responsibility ends. The Town says covenants can be stricter than Town code and that it generally cannot settle disputes between neighbors. Photograph damage, keep receipts and read your documents before you authorize a repair. Plumbing repairs themselves follow the 2024 North Carolina Plumbing Code (based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code, IPC), which became mandatory on July 1, 2025.

Documenting a loss in a condo building.

When water damage crosses units, paper matters almost as much as the repair. Take dated photos before cleanup, save the plumber's written finding of where the leak started, and send both to your association and your own insurer. A condo owner's policy and the association's policy usually cover different things, so ask each what it covers rather than assuming.

Do not authorize demolition of a shared wall or ceiling until your manager has agreed in writing, because the building may have its own contractors and approval steps.

Leaks you cannot see.

In multi-family buildings a hidden leak can raise a bill or stain a ceiling before anyone sees water. A leak detection visit tests the lines and finds the source, which helps when you need to show a manager or an insurer where the water came from.
