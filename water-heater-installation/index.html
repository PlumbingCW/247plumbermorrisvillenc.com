Four ways to heat water at home.

Most households choose among four setups. Each makes sense in some homes and causes headaches in others, so the table lays out the honest trade-offs.

Water heater types compared.
Type.Best fit.Trade-off.
Gas tank.Homes with a gas line and a working flue.Needs venting and combustion air, and the tank can run out in a long shower.
Electric tank.Homes without gas, or closets with no way to vent.Slower recovery than gas, with higher running cost in many homes.
Heat pump (hybrid) tank.Homes with a roomy space and air to draw from.Needs clearance, a condensate drain and a suitable circuit. It also runs louder than a plain tank.
Tankless, gas or electric.Households that want endless hot water and wall space back.Needs a large gas line or a big circuit, and yearly flushing.

Tankless units get their own treatment on the tankless page. If you are replacing a failed tank with the same type, the replacement page is the better starting point.

Heat pump water heaters: space, airflow and incentives.

A heat pump unit pulls heat from the air around it and moves it into the tank. That means it needs open air, roughly the volume of a small utility room, and it makes a fan sound. It also cools the room it sits in. Tell us where you plan to put it and we will say honestly if the space works.

The federal 25C credit covered heat pump water heater installs through December 31, 2025, and the IRS says it is not available for 2026 installs. Duke Energy publishes a heat pump water heater rebate page, but we could not confirm that it applies in North Carolina or that it is open now. Ask Duke Energy before you plan around it. We do not quote rebate amounts.

The expansion tank, in plain words.

Water expands when it heats. If a check valve or pressure regulator makes your plumbing a closed system, that extra volume has nowhere to go and pressure spikes each time the heater fires. A small thermal expansion tank on the cold line soaks up the swing.

We check pressure with a gauge at a hose bib and look for the valve that closes the system. If the pressure climbs, the expansion tank goes in with the heater.

Venting, combustion air and the gas connection.

A gas heater is only as safe as its flue. The vent has to rise with a steady pitch, connect tight and end where exhaust can leave the house. A closet needs a way to draw combustion air. We also add a sediment trap and a shutoff on the gas line near the unit, and use a listed flex connector.

Electric and heat pump units skip the flue but need a circuit sized to the nameplate. When the panel is full or old, an electrician may need to be involved, and we tell you before the day of the install.

Code and permits for Morrisville installs.

North Carolina's 2024 Plumbing Code became mandatory on July 1, 2025. It is built on the 2021 International Plumbing Code with North Carolina amendments, and licensed plumbers install to it.

The Town of Morrisville Inspections Department says a plumbing permit is required for installing a plumbing system. A like-for-like water heater swap in a one or two family home is generally exempt, provided the fuel, location and routing of venting and piping stay the same. A new install, a fuel change or a relocation usually falls outside that. Confirm with the Inspections Department at 919-463-6190 before work starts.

Source: Town of Morrisville Residential Inspections page and ICC code update

Relocating a water heater.

Moving a heater from a closet to a garage, or from an attic to a ground-floor space, frees room and reduces damage risk. It also changes piping, venting and electrical work, which is why it sits on the permit side of the question and not the simple swap side.

Before you move a tank.

  • Pick a spot where a leak will find a drain, not finished flooring.
  • Check that a gas flue can reach the roof or wall with proper pitch.
  • Confirm the new location has combustion air and clearances.
  • Ask the Inspections Department whether the move needs a permit.
  • Plan a pan with a drain line to a visible spot.

What changes the price of a water heater installation.

Installations vary more than swaps because more can change. A clear price before work starts covers the full scope.

Type of heater.

Tank, heat pump and tankless units differ widely in equipment and labor.

New runs.

Gas pipe, vent runs, electric circuits or a condensate drain add materials and time.

Location.

A new spot may need new piping, a platform or a pan with a drain line.

Permits and inspections.

If the town requires a permit, the application and inspection time belong to the job.

Add-ons.

Expansion tanks, mixing valves and recirculation lines each add parts and labor.

Choosing a licensed installer.

North Carolina licenses plumbing contractors through the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors. The board's website has a public license search, and checking it takes two minutes. Our plumbers are licensed and insured.

A water heater touches gas, electricity, water and venting at once. Related work such as new fixtures or branch piping falls under plumbing installation, and a gas run is handled as gas line work.
