Durham water is not Morrisville water.

Durham's drinking water comes from Lake Michie and the Little River Reservoir, according to the city's water quality page, and the City of Durham runs the mains under your street. Morrisville is different. Our home town buys its water from the Town of Cary, so we cannot assume that what we know about a Morrisville service line applies a few miles up the road.

The practical rule is simple. If water is out or weak on the whole block, the problem is probably the city main and the number to call is 919-560-4344, which the city lists for water main breaks and sewer overflows. If only your house is affected, or you can see water pooling at your own meter box, that is the part we fix. Leak detection sorts out which side of the meter the water is on.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Durham.

Before we arrive.

Four steps limit the damage in the first ten minutes. They are the same whether the leak is a burst supply line or a failed water heater.

Close the main valve.

Look for the shutoff where the line enters the house, often by the water heater or on a front basement or garage wall, and turn it clockwise.

Kill power near water.

If water reaches outlets, appliances or a panel, switch the breaker off from a dry spot. Never wade into standing water to reach it.

Clear the area.

Move people, pets and valuables away from the wet floor and open the nearest drain or door if water is rising.

Permits for Durham plumbing work.

Durham's City-County Building and Safety Department handles permits and inspections for the city and the county. Its plumbing permit page says a permit is required for new construction, additions, alterations, replacements and all private utilities, meaning sewer and water services. Backflow device installations need a separate permit from Durham's Cross Connection Control Department.

In practice that means a water heater swap or a service line replacement in Durham is a permitted job, and the permit applicant has to schedule the inspection. Ask any plumber, including us, who pulls the permit before the work starts. For Durham addresses the department's office is at 101 City Hall Plaza and its phone is 919-560-4144.

What the 2026 main breaks mean for your house.

News reporting in September 2026 counted more than 141 water main breaks in Durham so far that year, and the city moved to Stage 2 water restrictions in June 2026 during a drought. Those are city-side events, but they change how homeowners should watch their own plumbing.

A main break can drop pressure and then restore it with a surge. After a surge, check under sinks, behind the water heater and around the meter for new drips. A meter that keeps turning with every tap closed means a leak somewhere past the meter. That is the moment to call about water line repair or a leak survey instead of waiting for the bill.

Sewers, plants and backups on the Durham side.

The City of Durham operates about 1,100 miles of sewer line and two water reclamation plants, North Durham and South Durham. Durham County runs the Triangle plant that serves Research Triangle Park. The plant that handles your waste depends on where you live, but the clog that backs up your tub is almost always in your own line.

If a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or two floor drains back up together, the blockage sits in the main drain. Roots, grease and wipes cause most of them. A cable machine or hydro jetting clears it, and a camera run afterwards shows whether the pipe is damaged. Call the city only if the cleanout in your yard is full and the street manhole is overflowing.
